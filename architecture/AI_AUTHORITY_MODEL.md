# TownBoss Portfolio AI Authority Model

**Status:** Foundation v1 — initial model; expand as capabilities mature

## Purpose

TownBoss products must distinguish what AI may suggest, prepare, execute, and what requires human or qualified-professional authority.

## Authority levels

### A0 — Inform / Explain
AI may summarize, retrieve, explain, compare, and surface evidence without changing authoritative state.

### A1 — Recommend / Draft
AI may generate recommendations, plans, scenarios, drafts, proposed classifications, or candidate actions. A human chooses whether to proceed.

### A2 — Prepare for approval
AI may prepare a consequential transaction or structured action but must stop before the authoritative mutation until the required approver accepts it.

### A3 — Bounded execution
AI may execute explicitly authorized, reversible or sufficiently controlled actions within deterministic constraints, permissions, audit logging, and recovery rules.

### A4 — Prohibited autonomous authority
AI must not independently claim or exercise authority reserved for government, licensed professionals, legal signatories, financial custodians, or other high-consequence decision makers where human/professional authorization is required.

## Cross-portfolio rules

- Authorization and tenancy constraints apply regardless of AI capability.
- Confidence is not authority.
- AI inference must not be presented as an authoritative record merely because confidence is high.
- Consequential actions should use simulation/preflight where appropriate before execution.
- Evidence/provenance should accompany important AI-derived claims and decisions where practical.
- Escalate to qualified humans/professionals when confidence or authority is insufficient.
- GeoPlotter/TownDeveloper generated plans and visualizations remain planning aids until relevant professionals validate them.
- Government systems remain authoritative for government records and services; GlenTown/Glen AI may orchestrate, preflight, explain, link, and track without implying government authority.
- Trading/financial automation requires explicit risk limits and its own domain-specific execution authority policy.

## Next expansion

Each registered AI-enabled capability should eventually declare its maximum default authority level, required approvals, reversible actions, prohibited actions, evidence requirements, and audit events.
